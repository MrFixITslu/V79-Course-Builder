# V79 Junior AI Academy — Instructor & Guardian Operating Guide

This guide supports the 16-week **V79 Junior AI Academy: AI Superpowers for Kids** for learners ages 6–12.

The programme is designed to teach transferable AI thinking and creation skills rather than dependence on one brand of AI product.

## 1. Adult supervision

Learners in this age range should use AI tools only through instructor-approved arrangements.

- Do not require children to create third-party AI accounts unless the service is approved for the learner’s age and the organisation has decided that the account model is appropriate.
- Prefer instructor-managed, institution-managed or otherwise approved access where possible.
- Keep the course vendor-neutral so a tool can be replaced without changing the learning objective.
- When a tool cannot be used safely or appropriately, complete the activity with teacher demonstration, paper prototyping, pre-generated examples or another approved tool.

## 2. Privacy and personal information

The programme uses **STOP Before You Prompt**:

- **S — Secrets:** do not put secrets in AI prompts.
- **T — Telephone / address:** do not share contact details, home addresses or precise personal location.
- **O — Online passwords:** never share passwords, login codes or recovery information.
- **P — Personal information:** share only what is safe, necessary and approved.

Learners should also avoid uploading private school records, private family information or identifying information about another child.

## 3. Faces, voices and permission

Before a learner uses another person’s face, voice, photograph, artwork or private information:

1. Ask whether it is necessary.
2. Get appropriate permission.
3. Explain how the material will be used.
4. Do not publish beyond the approved audience.
5. Do not imitate or clone a real person in a way that could mislead others.

Synthetic media that could reasonably be mistaken for a real event or real person should be presented honestly and labelled when needed.

## 4. AI honesty

Learners are taught that AI assistance is allowed when the activity permits it, but the learner remains responsible for the result.

They should be able to explain:

- what they asked the AI to do;
- what the AI produced;
- what they checked;
- what they changed;
- what ideas or decisions came from the learner/team; and
- where AI materially assisted the final work.

The goal is not to catch children “using AI.” The goal is to teach them to use it transparently and thoughtfully.

## 5. Team formation

The platform forms **AI Studio Teams of exactly three**.

Recommended roles:

- **Leader:** keeps the team focused, checks deadlines, gathers the weekly Check-In and performs the handover.
- **Builder:** helps turn ideas into the week’s artifact.
- **Checker:** checks accuracy, safety, quality and whether everyone was heard.

All three learners still participate in planning, creating and checking.

The Leader role should rotate during the programme so leadership is practiced by each learner.

A learner should not be treated as a permanent “weak member” or “strong member.” Roles are learning opportunities, not labels.

## 6. Weekly operating rhythm

Each mission follows:

**Discover → Create → Team Project → Check → Submit → Instructor Review → Improve**

A typical session can be run as:

1. **10–15 min:** mission introduction and concept.
2. **15–25 min:** individual/pair activity.
3. **25–40 min:** AI Studio Team project work.
4. **10 min:** task/risk update and individual reflections.
5. **5–10 min:** Team Leader prepares the Studio Check-In.

The exact timing can be shortened or extended to suit the programme format.

## 7. Project management for children

Use simple language first, then introduce the professional term.

| Child-friendly language | Project-management idea |
| --- | --- |
| What are we trying to make? | Goal / objective |
| What must we finish this week? | Milestone |
| Who owns this job? | Task ownership |
| When do we need it? | Deadline |
| To Do → Doing → Done | Work board / status |
| What could go wrong? | Risk |
| How can we stop it? | Prevention / mitigation |
| What will we do instead? | Backup / contingency plan |
| What needs to happen first? | Dependency |
| Are we really finished? | Definition of done / quality check |

For ages 6–8, call the risk activity the **Uh-Oh Plan**.  
For ages 9–12, introduce Low / Medium / High risk levels.

## 8. Conflict resolution

For normal disagreements, use **CALM**:

- **C — Cool down**
- **A — Ask & listen**
- **L — Look for fair choices**
- **M — Make an agreement**

The CALM card is not a substitute for adult intervention.

A learner should tell an adult immediately about:

- bullying;
- threats;
- physical safety concerns;
- harassment or discrimination;
- repeated exclusion;
- privacy violations;
- coercion;
- sexual content or sexualised behaviour;
- a learner being pressured to share private information; or
- any situation that feels unsafe.

Do not require children to “solve it themselves” when adult safeguarding is appropriate.

## 9. Weekly instructor review

Every learner saves an individual reflection:

- What did I help with?
- What did I learn?
- What will I do next?

The current Team Leader submits the team Studio Check-In.

Recommended feedback:

- **⭐ Strong** — something the team did well.
- **🔧 Improve** — one clear change that matters.
- **🚀 Next** — the priority for the next iteration.

Use **Needs Changes** when revision would materially improve the learning or deliverable.  
Use **Approved** when the weekly milestone is complete enough to move forward.

Revision is part of the course design and should not be framed as failure.

## 10. Grading

Suggested 1–4 rubric dimensions for older learners:

1. Skill / Understanding
2. Quality / Accuracy
3. Teamwork / Communication
4. Responsibility / Project Management
5. Safety / Ethics

For younger learners, the same ideas can be expressed as:

- 🌟 Great job
- 👍 On track
- 🔧 Needs a little work
- 🤝 Needs instructor help

The written feedback is more important than the number.

## 11. Certificate requirement

The Junior Academy certificate is intentionally stricter than simple lesson completion.

A learner must have:

- completed all course lessons;
- belonged to a Studio Team;
- all 16 weekly Studio Check-Ins approved; and
- their own individual reflection saved for all 16 missions.

This protects individual accountability inside team work.

## 13. Final Demo Day

The final team presentation should answer:

1. What did we make?
2. Who is it for?
3. What problem or opportunity did we choose?
4. What did we research and verify?
5. How did AI help?
6. What did humans change or decide?
7. What risk did we manage?
8. What disagreement or challenge did we handle?
9. What did each learner contribute?
10. What would we improve next time?

The final showcase should reward explanation, evidence, teamwork and growth—not just visual polish.
