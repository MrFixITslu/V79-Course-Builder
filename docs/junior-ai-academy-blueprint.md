# V79 Junior AI Academy — Curriculum Blueprint

**Working title:** V79 Junior AI Academy: AI Superpowers for Kids  
**Audience:** Ages 6–12  
**Delivery:** Afterschool program + self-paced subscription course  
**Core levels:** Level 1 AI Explorer → Level 2 AI Creator → Level 3 AI Builder  
**Future course:** Advanced app building and deployment is intentionally excluded from this course.

## 1. Course promise

By the end of the core program, learners should be able to:

- Explain AI in age-appropriate language and recognize common ways AI is used.
- Give AI clear instructions and improve prompts through iteration.
- Protect private information and use AI safely, honestly and respectfully.
- Research with AI without treating AI output as automatically true.
- Verify important claims using trustworthy sources.
- Create images, stories, audio, short videos, presentations and promotional content with AI assistance.
- Combine multiple AI tools into a simple creative workflow.
- Use AI to solve a school, community or customer problem.
- Build and present a small project or supervised mini-business concept.
- Explain what the AI contributed and what the human creator contributed.
- Build a personal AI portfolio of completed work.
- Work effectively in a team of three with clear roles and shared responsibility.
- Take turns leading a team and reporting progress.
- Use age-appropriate planning, task ownership, milestones, deadlines and risk management.
- Handle normal disagreements using the CALM conflict-resolution method and know when to ask an adult for help.
- Submit weekly team work for instructor grading/review, respond to feedback and revise.
- Build one team project from Week 1 into a finished product for the final showcase.
- Identify careers and entrepreneurial paths where these skills can be useful.

## 2. Two age paths inside one course

The whole group follows the same weekly theme, but activities scale by age.

### AI Explorers — ages 6–8
- Very short explanations.
- Picture-led instructions.
- Teacher-led or pair-based prompting.
- More speaking, drawing, matching and choosing.
- 1–3 step projects.
- Teacher-provided research sources.
- Pretend shop/business activities.
- Short presentations.

### AI Creators — ages 9–12
- More independent prompting.
- Structured research and source comparison.
- Multi-step projects.
- Greater responsibility for planning and revision.
- Marketing, audience and customer concepts.
- Portfolio-quality outputs.
- More detailed reflection about ethics, copyright, misinformation and privacy.

## 3. The V79 AI Creator Code

Every level reinforces the same six-step habit:

1. **THINK** — What am I trying to do?
2. **ASK** — Give AI clear instructions.
3. **CHECK** — Is the result correct, safe and appropriate?
4. **CREATE** — Improve it and add your own ideas.
5. **BE RESPONSIBLE** — Protect privacy, respect people and be honest.
6. **SHARE** — Present your work clearly and responsibly.

## 4. Prompting framework: MAGIC

A simple child-friendly framework:

- **M — Mission:** What do you want the AI to do?
- **A — Audience:** Who is this for?
- **G — Give details:** What must it include?
- **I — Imagine the style:** What should it look/sound/feel like?
- **C — Check & Change:** What should be improved?

## 5. Safety rule: STOP Before You Prompt

- **S — Secrets**
- **T — Telephone/address**
- **O — Online passwords**
- **P — Personal information**

Children should learn to stop and check before entering private information into any AI tool.

## 6. Recurring characters

These characters make abstract ideas memorable and can appear in lesson art, short videos, quizzes and activities.

- **Pixel** — a friendly AI robot. Helpful, creative, sometimes confidently wrong.
- **Captain Verify** — the research detective who always asks, “How do we know?”
- **Shield** — the privacy and safety guardian.
- **Nova** — a young inventor/creator who demonstrates projects and entrepreneurship.

## 7. Standard mission format

Each weekly mission is designed for approximately 75–90 minutes of instructor-led afterschool time.

1. **Wow Moment — 5–10 min**  
   A surprising demo, image, short video or challenge.

2. **Discover — 10–15 min**  
   One simple concept. Avoid long lectures.

3. **Create Together — 10–15 min**  
   Instructor and learners complete a small example.

4. **Studio Team Mission — 25–35 min**  
   Learners work in teams of three. Each team has an accountable leader plus Builder and Checker responsibilities. The weekly task also advances the team's long-running final project.

5. **Check & Ethics Challenge — 5–10 min**  
   Accuracy, safety, fairness, privacy or honesty question.

6. **Show & Share — 5–10 min**  
   Learners explain what they made and one thing they changed.

7. **Badge / Knowledge Check — 5 min**  
   Short quiz, reflection or challenge.

8. **Weekly Studio Check-In**  
   Each team submits its weekly deliverable, leader report, task-board update, risk/problem check and individual contribution reflections for instructor review. Teams may receive **Needs Changes** and revise before approval.

## Teamwork, leadership, project management and conflict resolution

Learners work in **AI Studio Teams of three** throughout the program. The long-running team project begins in Week 1 and grows every week until the Week 16 showcase.

There is one Team Leader at a time. Leadership rotates by project phase so each learner experiences accountability and handover. Teams also use Creator/Builder and Checker/Researcher responsibilities.

Project management is deliberately simple:
- Goal.
- Milestones.
- To Do / Doing / Done.
- Task owner.
- Deadline.
- Risk.
- Backup plan.

For ages 6–8, risk management is introduced as the **Uh-Oh Plan**: “What could go wrong? How can we stop it? Who can help us?”

For ages 9–12, teams use simple Low / Medium / High risk cards with prevention and backup actions.

Normal disagreements use the **CALM Team Method**:
- **C — Cool down**
- **A — Ask & listen**
- **L — Look for fair choices**
- **M — Make an agreement**

Bullying, threats, harassment, repeated exclusion, unsafe behavior, discrimination or privacy problems go directly to an instructor rather than being handled only by the team.

See `docs/junior-ai-academy-team-project-model.md` for the full team, weekly review, grading and final-project model.

## 8. Three-level curriculum

# LEVEL 1 — AI EXPLORER

Goal: Understand AI, communicate with it, use it safely and learn not to accept everything it says as fact.

## Mission 1 — Welcome to the World of AI
**Big question:** What is AI and where do we already see it?

Micro-lessons:
1. Meet Pixel: What is AI?
2. Human or AI? What AI can and cannot do.
3. The V79 AI Creator Code.

Activities:
- AI-or-not guessing game.
- Sort examples into “AI may be involved” and “mostly human rules/tools.”
- Give Pixel a simple instruction and compare different results.
- Form AI Studio Teams of three.
- Choose a team name and create the Team Charter.
- Choose the first Team Leader.
- Brainstorm the team's long-running final project idea.
- Draw “an AI helper I wish existed.”

Deliverable:
- My AI Explorer passport/profile.
- Team Charter + first project idea + first Weekly Studio Check-In.

Badge:
- **AI Explorer**

## Mission 2 — Prompt Power
**Big question:** How do we tell AI what we really want?

Micro-lessons:
1. Bad prompt vs better prompt.
2. The MAGIC prompt.
3. Improve, retry and refine.

Activities:
- Prompt makeover challenge.
- Build a silly creature using increasingly detailed prompts.
- Prompt relay in teams.

Deliverable:
- Three versions of one prompt showing improvement.

Badge:
- **Prompt Wizard**

## Mission 3 — Safe, Smart & Ethical AI
**Big question:** What should we never share or ask AI to do?

Micro-lessons:
1. STOP Before You Prompt.
2. Ask permission and respect other people.
3. AI help vs cheating, copying and pretending.

Activities:
- Safe-to-share / not-safe-to-share card sort.
- “Would you do this?” ethics scenarios.
- Create a personal AI Safety Shield.

Deliverable:
- AI Safety Shield / Creator Promise.

Badge:
- **AI Safety Hero**

## Mission 4 — Become a Fact Detective
**Big question:** How do we know if AI is right?

Micro-lessons:
1. AI can make mistakes.
2. Captain Verify’s three questions: Who says? Where is the evidence? Is it current?
3. Compare sources before deciding.

Activities:
- Catch Pixel being wrong.
- Check a claim against instructor-approved sources.
- Fact / opinion / guess sorting game.

Deliverable:
- Fact Detective evidence card.

Badge:
- **Fact Detective**

## Mission 5 — AI Everywhere: Possibilities & Future Careers
**Big question:** What can people use AI skills for?

Micro-lessons:
1. AI at school, home, work and in the community.
2. Jobs that use AI as a tool.
3. What problem would you like to solve?

Activities:
- Career matching game.
- “How could AI help a…?” challenge: teacher, scientist, designer, farmer, doctor, filmmaker, entrepreneur, engineer.
- Future Me poster.

Deliverable:
- My Future-with-AI profile.

Badge:
- **Future Thinker**

# LEVEL 2 — AI CREATOR

Goal: Use AI as a creative partner while keeping human judgment, originality and responsibility central.

## Mission 6 — AI Image Studio
**Big question:** How do words become images?

Micro-lessons:
1. Subject + action + place + style + mood + details.
2. Composition, colour and audience.
3. Image ethics: fake images, permission and misleading content.

Activities:
- Invent a new animal.
- Create a book cover or environmental poster.
- Compare three prompt variations.

Deliverable:
- Finished poster/character plus prompt notes.

Badge:
- **Image Creator**

## Mission 7 — Story & Writing Lab
**Big question:** How can AI help us tell better stories without taking over our ideas?

Micro-lessons:
1. Idea → character → problem → solution.
2. Ask AI for options, not ownership.
3. Edit for your own voice.

Activities:
- Story dice + AI brainstorming.
- Create an illustrated short story.
- Rewrite a boring paragraph in three styles.

Deliverable:
- Illustrated story or mini comic.

Badge:
- **Story Director**

## Mission 8 — AI Audio Studio
**Big question:** How can AI help us plan and create sound?

Micro-lessons:
1. Voice, music, sound effects and scripts.
2. Podcast/radio structure.
3. Voice cloning and permission.

Activities:
- Create a 20–30 second radio advertisement.
- Plan a mini podcast.
- Build a sound-scene storyboard.

Deliverable:
- Short audio project or scripted audio package.

Badge:
- **Sound Designer**

## Mission 9 — AI Video Studio
**Big question:** How do we turn ideas into short videos?

Micro-lessons:
1. Storyboard: beginning, middle, end.
2. Visuals + narration + sound + captions.
3. Deepfakes, edited media and honesty.

Activities:
- Create a 4–6 scene storyboard.
- Make a short educational/promotional video.
- Spot signs that media may be synthetic or edited.

Deliverable:
- Short video and storyboard.

Badge:
- **Video Creator**

## Mission 10 — Presentation Power
**Big question:** How do we explain an idea so people understand and remember it?

Micro-lessons:
1. One main idea per slide.
2. Show, don’t overcrowd.
3. Speak to people, not to the screen.

Activities:
- Fix a terrible slide.
- Turn research into 3–8 slides depending on age.
- Present a one-minute idea.

Deliverable:
- Mini presentation.

Badge:
- **Presentation Pro**

## Mission 11 — Content Creator & Promotion Lab
**Big question:** How do we make people notice an idea without tricking them?

Micro-lessons:
1. Audience, message and call to action.
2. Posters, captions, thumbnails, scripts and short promotions.
3. Ethical promotion: persuade without lying.

Activities:
- Create a campaign for a school event, club, community cause or pretend product.
- Create a poster + caption + short video/audio script.
- Compare content for different audiences.

Deliverable:
- Mini promotional content pack.

Badge:
- **Content Creator**

# LEVEL 3 — AI BUILDER

Goal: Combine AI skills into useful workflows, solve problems and create a portfolio-ready project or supervised mini-business concept.

## Mission 12 — AI Workflow Wizard
**Big question:** How can different AI skills work together?

Micro-lessons:
1. Break a big task into smaller steps.
2. Choose the right tool for each step.
3. Review before moving information from one step to the next.

Activities:
- Build a workflow: research → script → image → audio/video → presentation.
- Find weak points in a bad workflow.
- Create a reusable workflow card.

Deliverable:
- Personal AI workflow map.

Badge:
- **Workflow Wizard**

## Mission 13 — AI Problem Solver
**Big question:** What real problem can we help solve?

Micro-lessons:
1. Understand the problem before creating.
2. Ask people what they need.
3. Prototype, test and improve.

Activities:
- Pick a school/community problem.
- Research it.
- Brainstorm solutions with AI.
- Create a simple prototype/campaign.

Deliverable:
- Problem → evidence → solution poster/presentation.

Badge:
- **Problem Solver**

## Mission 14 — Young AI Entrepreneur
**Big question:** Can our skills create something useful for someone else?

Micro-lessons:
1. Customer + problem + useful offer.
2. Name, brand and simple pricing/value concepts.
3. Responsibility when selling or promoting.

Activities:
- Pretend shop for younger learners.
- Mini-business concept for older learners.
- Create name, logo, product/service description and target customer.

Possible supervised ideas:
- Invitations.
- Posters.
- Storybooks.
- Colouring pages.
- Presentations.
- Social graphics.
- Simple promotional videos.
- Voiceovers.
- School/community campaign materials.

Deliverable:
- One-page mini-business concept.

Badge:
- **Young Entrepreneur**

## Mission 15 — Final Production Sprint
**Big question:** Can your team bring sixteen weeks of work together into one finished product?

This is not the beginning of the project. Teams have been building the project since Week 1.

Learners choose a project such as:
- Help visitors learn about Saint Lucia.
- Protect sea turtles or reefs.
- Promote a school event.
- Create a children’s story.
- Design a healthy snack brand.
- Teach children about hurricanes.
- Build a campaign for a community issue.
- Design a dream game concept.

Required steps:
1. Define the idea/problem.
2. Research and verify.
3. Plan the audience.
4. Create visuals.
5. Create written content.
6. Add audio/video where appropriate.
7. Build a presentation.
8. Create a promotional piece.
9. Explain what AI did and what the learner did.

Deliverable:
- Final multi-format team product.
- Completed task board and risk review.
- Individual contribution reflection.
- Instructor review submission.

Badge:
- **AI Builder**

## Mission 16 — AI Creator Showcase & Portfolio
**Big question:** How do we show what we know and what we can create?

Micro-lessons:
1. Choose your strongest work.
2. Explain your process.
3. Give and receive helpful feedback.

Activities:
- Portfolio setup.
- Practice a short team pitch.
- Explain roles, one project risk and one challenge/disagreement the team successfully handled.
- Parent/student demo day.
- Peer celebration.
- Individual reflection on contribution, leadership and teamwork.

Deliverable:
- V79 AI Portfolio + final team product + final presentation + individual reflection.

Badge:
- **V79 AI Creator**

## 9. Portfolio outcomes

Each learner should finish with examples of:

- Improved prompts.
- Safety/ethics reflection.
- Verified research.
- AI-generated or AI-assisted image project.
- Story/comic.
- Audio project.
- Short video.
- Presentation.
- Promotional content pack.
- Workflow map.
- Problem-solving project.
- Entrepreneurial concept.
- Final capstone.

## 10. Assessment approach

Do not make the course feel exam-heavy.

Use:
- Tiny knowledge checks.
- Show-me challenges.
- Weekly team submissions.
- Instructor grading/review and resubmission.
- Project rubrics.
- Individual contribution reflections.
- Team Leader status reports.
- Collaboration and responsibility observations.
- Risk/problem check-ins.
- Reflection questions.
- Peer explanation.
- Instructor observation.
- Portfolio evidence.

Recommended completion standard:
- Complete the required core missions.
- Participate in an AI Studio Team of three where possible.
- Complete required individual reflections.
- Submit required Weekly Studio Check-Ins.
- Respond to required instructor revisions.
- Complete the team's final product.
- Demonstrate individual contribution.
- Present at the final showcase.

## 11. Media approach

The course should be visual and active.

### For each micro-lesson
Target:
- 1 primary lesson illustration.
- 1 supporting diagram/example where useful.
- 1 activity visual/card where useful.
- A short 30–90 second lesson intro or explanation video for core concepts.

### For each weekly mission
Target:
- 1 mission thumbnail.
- 1 badge.
- 1 recurring-character scene.
- 1 printable or downloadable activity sheet.

### Video style
- Short.
- Captioned.
- Bright and friendly.
- One concept per video.
- Recurring characters.
- End with a question/challenge rather than a long summary.

Example:
**Pixel Says Penguins Live in Saint Lucia!**
1. Pixel confidently makes a false claim.
2. Captain Verify arrives.
3. Children are asked what they should check.
4. Video ends: “Your mission: prove Pixel right or wrong.”

## 12. Subscription model

The subscription should feel like an ongoing club, not a one-time locked course.

Core subscription:
- 16-mission foundational program.
- Portfolio.
- Badges/certificate.
- Parent showcase.

Ongoing subscriber content can later include:
- Monthly AI art challenge.
- AI science challenge.
- Comic challenge.
- Mini brand challenge.
- 60-second movie challenge.
- Research detective challenge.
- Holiday/seasonal projects.
- New tool demonstrations that do not require rewriting the entire core curriculum.

## 13. Tool philosophy

Avoid building the curriculum around one vendor.

Teach transferable skills:
- Prompting.
- Verification.
- Privacy.
- Creative direction.
- Editing.
- Storyboarding.
- Presentation.
- Audience awareness.
- Workflow planning.
- Problem solving.

Specific tools may change; the skill remains.

## 14. Platform implementation notes

The existing V79 Course Builder supports:
- Courses.
- Modules.
- Lessons.
- Learning objectives.
- Markdown/HTML lesson content.
- Video URLs.
- Audio URLs.
- Image URLs.
- Downloads.
- Exercises.
- Quizzes.
- Assignments.
- Student progress.
- Subscription access.

Recommended V79 mapping:
- **Course:** V79 Junior AI Academy.
- **Module:** One weekly mission.
- **Lessons:** 2–3 micro-lessons per mission.
- **Exercise prompt:** Creator Mission.
- **Quiz:** Badge/knowledge check.
- **Assignment:** Weekly Studio Check-In plus artifact/portfolio submission.
- **Images/video/audio:** Lesson media assets.
- **Pricing type:** Subscription.

Junior Academy requires additional platform work beyond the current individual assignment storage:
- Teams of three.
- Team membership and role history.
- Current Team Leader.
- Long-running team project.
- Weekly team submissions.
- Instructor review/grading states.
- Rubric feedback.
- Resubmission workflow.
- Individual contribution reflections.
- Team task board.
- Risk log.
- Optional conflict-resolution reflection.

The detailed proposed data/workflow model is in `docs/junior-ai-academy-team-project-model.md`.
