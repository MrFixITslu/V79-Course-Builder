# Mission 1 Prototype — Welcome to the World of AI

**Level:** 1 — AI Explorer  
**Recommended session:** 75–90 minutes  
**Badge:** AI Explorer  
**Characters:** Pixel, Nova  
**Main question:** What is AI, what can it do, and what is still our job as humans?

## Mission outcome

By the end of Mission 1, a learner should be able to:

- Say in simple words that AI is technology trained to find patterns and create/predict responses.
- Name at least three places where AI may be used.
- Explain that AI is not a person and does not “know” things the same way humans do.
- Give an AI system a simple instruction.
- Explain that humans still need to think, check and make decisions.
- Recite or recognize the V79 AI Creator Code.

# Lesson 1.1 — Meet Pixel: What Is AI?

## Kid-friendly explanation

AI is a kind of computer technology that learns patterns from lots of examples.

It can help people:
- Answer questions.
- Recognize pictures.
- Suggest music or videos.
- Create stories and pictures.
- Translate languages.
- Find patterns in information.

But AI is not a magical brain.

It can be useful.
It can be creative.
It can also make mistakes.

## Wow moment

Show Pixel generating three wildly different ideas from the same simple request:

> “Invent a new pet.”

Ask:
- Which idea is your favourite?
- Did Pixel decide what you like?
- Who should make the final choice: Pixel or you?

## Explorer activity — ages 6–8

### AI or Not?
Show picture cards:
- Voice assistant.
- Calculator.
- Pencil.
- Face filter.
- Recommendation feed.
- Toaster.
- Image generator.
- Search suggestion.

Children hold up:
- 🤖 “AI might be helping”
- 🧰 “Regular tool”
- ❓ “Not sure”

Important: Some real products combine ordinary software rules and AI. The goal is discussion, not trick questions.

## Creator activity — ages 9–12

Learners list five technologies they used this week.

For each:
1. Could AI be involved?
2. What might the AI be trying to predict/create?
3. What information might it use?
4. What should a human still decide?

## Check yourself

Which statement is best?

A. AI is always correct.  
B. AI is a tool that can help people, but people still need to think and check.  
C. AI is a person living inside the computer.

Correct: **B**

# Lesson 1.2 — Human or AI? What Can AI Do?

## Kid-friendly explanation

AI can be very fast at some tasks.

It can:
- Suggest ideas.
- Find patterns.
- Rewrite text.
- Make pictures.
- Summarize information.
- Help organize a plan.

Humans are still responsible for:
- Deciding what matters.
- Caring about people.
- Knowing the real situation around us.
- Checking whether information is true.
- Choosing what is fair and kind.
- Taking responsibility for decisions.

## Team challenge

Give teams these tasks:
- Make a birthday card for your best friend.
- Decide whether a classmate needs help.
- List 20 names for a superhero.
- Check whether a storm warning is real.
- Draw 10 logo ideas quickly.
- Decide whether a joke will hurt someone’s feelings.

For each task, choose:
- Human first.
- AI can help.
- Human must check.
- AI should not decide alone.

Discuss why.

## Explorer activity — ages 6–8

Draw two boxes:

**Pixel is good at…**  
**People are good at…**

Learners draw or paste pictures into each.

## Creator activity — ages 9–12

Choose one real task and design the best teamwork:
1. What does the human do first?
2. What can AI help with?
3. What must be checked?
4. Who makes the final decision?

## Ethics moment

Ask:

> If AI creates something that hurts someone, can we say “It wasn’t me — AI did it”?

Key idea:
**The person using the tool is still responsible for how they use the result.**

## Media assets

- Comparison graphic: “AI strengths / Human strengths / Best together.”
- Activity card set for the team challenge.
- Short video: Nova and Pixel try to plan a surprise party; Pixel suggests something unsuitable because it does not know the friend personally.

# Lesson 1.3 — Become an AI Creator

## The V79 AI Creator Code

Every time we use AI:

### 1. THINK
What am I trying to do?

### 2. ASK
Give AI clear instructions.

### 3. CHECK
Is the result correct, safe and useful?

### 4. CREATE
Improve it. Add your own ideas.

### 5. BE RESPONSIBLE
Protect privacy. Respect people. Be honest.

### 6. SHARE
Explain and present your work.

## Class call-and-response

Instructor:
> “Do we believe everything AI says?”

Students:
> “CHECK IT!”

Instructor:
> “Do we share private information?”

Students:
> “STOP!”

Instructor:
> “Who is the creator?”

Students:
> “WE ARE!”

## AI Studio Team Launch

Form teams of three.

Each team:
1. Chooses a team name.
2. Chooses the first Team Leader.
3. Assigns Builder and Checker responsibilities.
4. Creates a Team Charter.
5. Learns the CALM conflict method.
6. Brainstorms one long-running project to develop across the course.

### CALM
- **Cool down**
- **Ask & listen**
- **Look for fair choices**
- **Make an agreement**

The team also creates its first simple board:

**To Do → Doing → Done**

And its first **Uh-Oh Plan**:
- What could go wrong?
- How can we stop it?
- Who can help us?

## Creator Mission

### My AI Helper

Invent an AI helper.

It can help with:
- School.
- Sports.
- Art.
- The environment.
- Pets.
- Community.
- Travel.
- Games.
- Something else.

Learners create:

**Name:**  
**What problem does it help with?**  
**What can AI do?**  
**What must a human still do?**  
**What information should it never ask for?**

### Ages 6–8
Draw the helper and answer orally or with short phrases.

### Ages 9–12
Create a one-page profile and write the first prompt they would give their AI helper.

## Share

Each learner gives a 20–60 second explanation.

## Mission badge check

Learner earns the **AI Explorer** badge after they can demonstrate:

- One thing AI can help with.
- One thing a human should still do.
- One reason AI results need checking.

## Suggested quiz

1. AI can sometimes make mistakes.  
   **True**

2. Who should make important final decisions?  
   A. AI alone  
   B. A responsible person using information and judgment  
   C. Nobody  
   **Correct: B**

3. Which is part of the V79 AI Creator Code?  
   A. Think, Ask, Check  
   B. Copy, Hide, Pretend  
   C. Believe everything  
   **Correct: A**
