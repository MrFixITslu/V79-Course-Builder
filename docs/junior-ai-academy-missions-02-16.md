# V79 Junior AI Academy — Missions 2–16 Instructor Plans

This document expands the seeded curriculum into instructor-ready weekly plans. Mission 1 is documented separately in `docs/junior-ai-academy-mission-01.md`.

Every week follows the same studio rhythm:

**Discover → Create → Team Project → Check → Submit → Instructor Review → Improve**

Every learner also completes an individual reflection:
- What did I help with?
- What did I learn?
- What will I do next week?

The current Team Leader submits the team’s Weekly Studio Check-In. Normal disagreements use **CALM**: Cool down → Ask & listen → Look for fair choices → Make an agreement.

## Mission 2 — Prompt Power
**Level:** AI Explorer  
**Badge:** Prompt Wizard  
**Big question:** How do we tell AI what we really want?

### Learning goals
- Explain what a prompt is.
- Use the MAGIC prompt: Mission, Audience, Give details, Imagine the style, Check & Change.
- Improve an AI result by revising the prompt instead of accepting the first answer.

### Ages 6–8
Use a picture-led MAGIC card to create a silly creature with at least three details. Compare a tiny prompt such as “make a monster” with a clearer version.

### Ages 9–12
Write a MAGIC prompt connected to the team project, test it, record at least two revisions and explain why the changes improved the result.

### Team project milestone
Create a shared **Team Prompt Bank** for brainstorming, researching, creating and checking.

### Project-management skill
Break one big goal into smaller tasks, assign an owner and choose a due week.

### Safety / ethics
Good prompting must not be used to harass, impersonate, deceive or create harmful material.

### Weekly submission
Three improved prompts + Team Prompt Bank + task-board update + risk update + individual reflections.

### Instructor review focus
Clarity of instructions, evidence of iteration, teamwork and task ownership.

## Mission 3 — Safe, Smart & Ethical AI
**Level:** AI Explorer  
**Badge:** AI Safety Hero  
**Big question:** How do we use AI without putting ourselves or other people at risk?

### Learning goals
- Apply STOP Before You Prompt.
- Recognize private information.
- Understand permission, honesty and responsible AI assistance.

### Ages 6–8
Sort examples into “safe to share,” “ask an adult first,” and “do not share.” Create an AI Safety Shield.

### Ages 9–12
Review realistic school and creator scenarios. Explain whether each is safe, unfair, misleading or private.

### Team project milestone
Create the team **Safety & Ethics Plan**, including privacy, permission, truthful claims and how AI assistance will be acknowledged.

### Project-management skill
Add safety and permission checks to the definition of done.

### Conflict skill
Practice saying: “I’m not comfortable sharing that information. Let’s ask the instructor.”

### Weekly submission
Safety Shield / scenario reflection + Team Safety & Ethics Plan.

### Instructor review focus
Privacy understanding, safe decision making and whether the project plan avoids unnecessary personal data.

## Mission 4 — Become a Fact Detective
**Level:** AI Explorer  
**Badge:** Fact Detective  
**Big question:** How do we know if an AI answer is actually true?

### Learning goals
- Explain that AI can confidently make mistakes.
- Ask: Who says this? Where is the evidence? Is it current?
- Compare claims with reliable sources.

### Ages 6–8
Use two teacher-approved sources to check a simple claim.

### Ages 9–12
Research one team-project question using multiple sources. Record the source, date, claim and what the source actually supports.

### Team project milestone
Build a **Research Pack** containing the verified facts the project will rely on.

### Project-management skill
Track unanswered research questions as tasks rather than guessing.

### Safety / ethics
Do not repeat dramatic claims simply because AI produced them.

### Weekly submission
Fact Detective evidence card + Research Pack.

### Instructor review focus
Source quality, factual support, current information where needed, and distinction between fact/opinion/guess.

# LEVEL 2 — AI CREATOR

## Mission 6 — AI Image Studio
**Badge:** Image Creator  
**Big question:** How do words become useful images?

### Learning goals
- Build image prompts using subject, action, place, style, mood and details.
- Compare variations and choose intentionally.
- Recognize the risk of misleading synthetic images.

### Ages 6–8
Invent a new animal, hero or project mascot using a guided prompt.

### Ages 9–12
Create a poster, character or visual identity for the team project and document prompt revisions.

### Team project milestone
Approve the project’s visual direction and first production visual.

### Project-management skill
Create a simple **definition of done**: correct size, readable, suitable for audience, checked by another teammate.

### Safety / ethics
Do not present a synthetic image of a real person or event as proof that it happened.

### Weekly submission
Finished visual + prompt notes + visual-direction decision.

### Instructor review focus
Prompt quality, visual purpose, originality, audience fit and honest use.

## Mission 7 — Story & Writing Lab
**Badge:** Story Director  
**Big question:** How can AI help us tell better stories without taking over our ideas?

### Learning goals
- Use a beginning / middle / end structure.
- Ask AI for options rather than surrendering creative control.
- Edit for audience, voice and clarity.

### Ages 6–8
Create a short illustrated story with a beginning, challenge and ending.

### Ages 9–12
Draft and revise a project script, article or narrative. Show what the team changed after AI assistance.

Create the project’s core message, story or script.

### Project-management skill
Use visible versions: Draft 1 → Feedback → Draft 2.

### Safety / ethics
Do not copy another creator’s work and claim it as original.

### Weekly submission
Draft + revised version + explanation of human edits.

### Instructor review focus
Structure, audience, human authorship, revision quality and shared contribution.

# LEVEL 3 — AI BUILDER

## Mission 12 — AI Workflow Wizard
**Badge:** Workflow Wizard  
**Big question:** How can different AI skills work together in one reliable process?

### Learning goals
- Explain a workflow as connected steps.
- Choose appropriate tools / people for different tasks.
- Use human checks between important steps.
- Identify dependencies.

### Ages 6–8
Arrange workflow cards and add a CHECK card between key stages.

### Ages 9–12
Create a workflow map with tools, owners, inputs, outputs, checks and dependencies.

### Team project milestone
Map the complete final-project workflow and identify unfinished parts.

### Project-management skill
Ask: “What cannot start until something else is finished?”

### Safety / ethics
More automation does not remove human responsibility.

### Weekly submission
Workflow map + dependency / bottleneck review.

### Instructor review focus
Logical sequence, ownership, checks, risks and handoffs.

Completeness, safety, factual accuracy, readiness and whether known problems were dealt with honestly.

## Mission 16 — AI Creator Showcase & Portfolio
**Badge:** V79 AI Creator  
**Big question:** How do we show what we created, how we worked and what we learned?

### Learning goals
- Build a portfolio showing outcomes and growth.
- Present the problem, process, evidence, decisions and product.
- Give fair credit.
- Reflect on teamwork, leadership, risks and conflict resolution.

### Ages 6–8
Use a show-and-tell card:
- We made…
- We learned…
- I helped by…

### Ages 9–12
Deliver a team demo and complete an individual reflection on contribution, leadership, feedback and next steps.

### Team project milestone
**Demo Day:** present the finished product and complete a team retrospective.

### Required presentation points
1. What problem / opportunity did we choose?
2. Who is the audience?
3. What did we research and verify?
4. How did AI help?
5. What did humans change or decide?
6. What project risk did we manage?
7. What challenge or disagreement did we solve?
8. What did each person contribute?
9. What would we improve with more time?

### Project-management skill
Retrospective:
- What went well?
- What was difficult?
- What would we change next time?

### Safety / ethics
Give teammates credit and say honestly where AI assisted.

### Weekly / final submission
Finished team product + final presentation + portfolio + approved Week 16 Studio Check-In + individual reflection.

### Certificate gate
For the Junior Academy, certificate eligibility requires:
- all lessons completed;
- all 16 Weekly Studio Check-Ins approved; and
- the learner’s individual reflection saved for every mission.

# Weekly grading rubric

For ages 6–8, instructors may show the same rubric as symbols:
- 🌟 Great job
- 👍 On track
- 🔧 Needs a little work
- 🤝 Needs instructor help

For ages 9–12, the system stores 1–4 ratings:

1. **Skill / Understanding**
2. **Quality / Accuracy**
3. **Teamwork / Communication**
4. **Responsibility / Project Management**
5. **Safety / Ethics** when relevant

Written feedback should remain the most important part:
- **⭐ Strong**
- **🔧 Improve**
- **🚀 Next**

A team may receive **Needs Changes**, revise the work, and resubmit. Revision is part of the learning model, not a punishment.
